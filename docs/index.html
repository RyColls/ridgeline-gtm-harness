The short version

Ridgeline gets 900 leads a month and takes 31 hours to call them back. The reps don't trust the lead scores, so they ignore them. I designed one system that cleans each lead, scores it with reasons a rep can read, and gets it to the right rep in seconds. Then it keeps the scores honest using the reps' own feedback.

The questions you'll ask

1What's actually wrong at Ridgeline?

It looks like five problems: slow follow-up, generic emails, missing data, duplicates and bad scores. It's really one chain. Missing and duplicate data make the scores wrong. Wrong scores teach reps to ignore them. Reps who ignore scores pick leads by gut and sit on the rest. That's the 31 hours.

So the first job is trust, not speed. Fast routing of a lead nobody trusts just gets ignored faster.

2Which motion did you pick, and why?

Both, on one shared pipeline (hybrid). Inbound and outbound need the same parts: a clean record, a score and an owner. Building them separately would recreate the "five tools that each think they're the truth" problem. The best signal also crosses both motions: a target account looking at the pricing page.

Inbound gets built first because it's the quickest provable win. The leads already exist; they're just waiting 31 hours.

3Why did you prototype scoring and not something else?

Everything else depends on it. Routing, the 15-minute alert and outbound priority all read the score. If reps don't believe the score, none of it matters, and the Head of SDR said they'll ignore it after two mistakes.

So the score is built to be argued with. Every point comes from one fact about the lead, the rep can see each fact, and RevOps owns the weights.

4How does a rep know why they got a lead?

Every lead has a trace: each step it went through, the score with its reasons, where each piece of data came from, and who it was sent to and why. AI writes a 2–3 sentence summary, but code checks every fact in it against the record first. If the AI made something up, the rep never sees it.

Example: the AI claimed Redwood Haul runs "about 300 trucks." We don't know their fleet size, so the note was blocked.

5Why say no to Marketing?

Marketing wants every content download sent to an SDR. Run through the scoring on a normal week, that's about 98 leads for 3 SDRs, and 78 of them are people who just read a report. That flood is exactly why reps stopped trusting leads.

What Marketing still gets: every download is captured, scored and credited to them. Only real buyers go to an SDR (about 42 a week). The rest are nurtured and move up automatically if they show real interest.

6How does the CRO see who's sitting on leads?

Each top lead starts a 15-minute clock when it's assigned. If nobody calls or emails in time, the SDR manager is alerted and the miss is logged against that rep. A weekly table shows each rep's leads, response time and misses. Facts, not anecdotes.

7Where is AI used, and why not more?

Rules make decisions; AI writes words. Scores, routing and deadlines must be the same every time and explainable to RevOps, so they're plain rules. AI does the work rules can't: writing the plain-English "why" for reps, drafting outbound emails and researching accounts. Each AI output passes a code check before anyone sees it.

8How do you stop outbound from being wrong or generic?

Every draft must point to something real about the account, like their dispatcher job posts or pricing-page visits, and may only quote approved customer results. A checker tests each draft. Small problems (a blank name field, pricing talk) get fixed automatically. A made-up fact blocks the email for a person to review.

0The reframe: it's a trust problem, not a speed problem

In shortRidgeline's five complaints are one problem. The data is messy, so scores are wrong, so reps stop trusting leads, so they respond slowly. Fix trust first and speed follows.

The kickoff list reads like five separate problems: slow speed-to-lead, generic outbound, low enrichment, duplicates, untrusted scores. They're one loop:

Bad data (30% enriched, 22% duplicates) → wrong scores → reps stop trusting the system → reps cherry-pick and sit on leads → 31-hour speed-to-lead → no feedback reaches the scores → scores stay wrong.

Making routing faster without fixing trust just delivers untrusted leads faster. So the design is ordered around trust: one clean record first, a score anyone can read and argue with, an SLA that makes rep behavior visible, and a feedback loop that turns "this is wrong" into a rule change within a week. Speed falls out of that.

Three design principles follow, and every step below obeys them:

  1. The CRM is the system of record; the harness is the system of reasoning. Contacts, accounts, owners and activities live in the CRM. Why something happened (traces, score versions, eval results, feedback) lives in the harness log.
  2. One writer per field. Every CRM field has exactly one system allowed to write it. Rep edits lock a field from automated overwrite. This is how the five tools stop each thinking they're the source of truth.
  3. LLMs write words; rules make decisions. Tiers, routes, SLAs and send/no-send are deterministic and versioned. AI drafts explanations and messages, and code checks them before anyone sees them.

1Motion: hybrid, built inbound-first

In shortInbound and outbound need the same parts (clean record, score, owner), so I built one pipeline for both. Inbound goes first because 900 leads a month waiting 31 hours is the fastest money to recover.

Hybrid, because Ridgeline's strongest buying signal crosses the two motions: a named target account visiting the pricing page is better intent than most form fills, and today nobody acts on it. Inbound and outbound also need exactly the same parts (clean record, enrichment, score, owner), so building them separately would recreate the "five tools" problem.

Inbound first, because it's the fastest provable win: 900 form fills a month and a 31-hour baseline means a measurable result in weeks. It also forces the shared spine (enrichment, dedupe, scoring, CRM writeback) to exist, which outbound then reuses.

Trust: a score a rep can argue with

I prototyped scoring because every other step depends on it: routing, SLAs, outbound priority and the SDRs' willingness to use any of it. Ridgeline's reps already ignore one score. This one is rules, not a model, so every point traces to a field, a source and a config version, and the AI only writes the sentence a rep reads, under a check.

Score a new lead View weights

1 · Explainable

Score = sum of named signals. Each reason cites a record field, its source and confidence. Unknown fields score 0 and are flagged, never guessed.

2 · Versioned

Weights live in scoring_v1_3.json, owned by RevOps. Every stored score carries its version, and any change is explained signal-by-signal against the prior version.

3 · Checked AI

The LLM writes the plain-English "why". Code verifies every sentence cites a real, non-empty field and every number appears in it. Fail → template reasons ship instead.
