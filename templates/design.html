Design · the main event

One spine, two motions, one log

A GTM harness for Ridgeline Freight Software: enrichment, scoring, routing, messaging and logging working as one system with one record per person and account. Hybrid motion; inbound built first; scoring prototyped.

0The reframe: it's a trust problem, not a speed problem

The kickoff list reads like five separate problems: slow speed-to-lead, generic outbound, low enrichment, duplicates, untrusted scores. They're one loop:

Bad data (30% enriched, 22% duplicates) → wrong scores → reps stop trusting the system → reps cherry-pick and sit on leads → 31-hour speed-to-lead → no feedback reaches the scores → scores stay wrong.

Making routing faster without fixing trust just delivers untrusted leads faster. So the design is ordered around trust: one clean record first, a score anyone can read and argue with, an SLA that makes rep behavior visible, and a feedback loop that turns "this is wrong" into a rule change within a week. Speed falls out of that.

Three design principles follow, and every step below obeys them:

  1. The CRM is the system of record; the harness is the system of reasoning. Contacts, accounts, owners and activities live in the CRM. Why something happened (traces, score versions, eval results, feedback) lives in the harness log.
  2. One writer per field. Every CRM field has exactly one system allowed to write it. Rep edits lock a field from automated overwrite. This is how the five tools stop each thinking they're the source of truth.
  3. LLMs write words; rules make decisions. Tiers, routes, SLAs and send/no-send are deterministic and versioned. AI drafts explanations and messages, and code checks them before anyone sees them.

1Motion: hybrid, built inbound-first

Hybrid, because Ridgeline's strongest buying signal crosses the two motions: a named target account visiting the pricing page is better intent than most form fills, and today nobody acts on it. Inbound and outbound also need exactly the same parts (clean record, enrichment, score, owner), so building them separately would recreate the "five tools" problem.

Inbound first, because it's the fastest provable win: 900 form fills a month and a 31-hour baseline means a measurable result in weeks. It also forces the shared spine (enrichment, dedupe, scoring, CRM writeback) to exist, which outbound then reuses.

Build order

Week 1–2Foundation
CRM field audit and ownership map; dedupe backfill; trace log; intake webhook with idempotency. Exit: one record per person, every inbound event logged.
Week 2–4Inbound spine
Junk filter, enrichment waterfall, scoring v1.3 in shadow mode for 2 weeks (scored, shown to reps, not routed). Exit: Tier A precision ≥ 70% on rep feedback.
Week 4–6Route + SLA
Routing rules live, Slack alerts with why notes, SLA monitor and escalation, rep table for the CRO. Exit: median Tier A speed-to-lead under 10 minutes.
Week 7–10Hybrid + outbound
Pricing-page signal play, signal store (hiring, funding), outbound drafts behind the eval gate with 100% human review at first, dropping to sampling once pass precision holds. Exit: grounded outbound A/B-tested against today's sequences.
OngoingLoop
Weekly feedback review; config releases via pull request; nightly re-score of nurture.

Named conflicts

  • Marketing volume vs SDR precision. Resolved by separating capture and credit (every download, Marketing's metric) from routing (only buyers, SDR's metric).
  • CRO speed vs RevOps explainability. Resolved in favor of explainable rules; speed comes from automation, not from a smarter opaque model.
  • CRO "different outbound" vs Head of SDR "don't be wrong". Personalization is the riskiest place for AI to be wrong, so outbound fails closed behind the eval gate and starts with full human review.

7Where AI fits, and where it doesn't

StepApproachWhy
Junk filter, dedupe, routing, SLARulesMust be instant, free, auditable and identical every time.
Persona from job titleRules (keyword map)Covers ~95% of titles; an LLM fallback for odd titles is a later option.
EnrichmentRules waterfall + one agentProviders are cheap and confident for most fields. A site-research agent only handles the long tail (fleet size from the company site), with a confidence score and a cost cap.
ScoringRulesRevOps must explain every point; LLM scores drift and can't be diffed. This is the RevOps ask.
Why note for repsLLM + deterministic checkPlain English is what makes reps read the reasons. The check guarantees every sentence cites a real, non-empty field and every number exists in it; else the template ships.
Outbound researchAgentReading sites, posts and news is unstructured work; every signal it returns must carry a source.
First-touch draftsLLMThe CRO wants non-generic copy; constrained to cited signals + approved stories.
Eval gateRules first, LLM judge secondCode catches uncited claims, numbers, merge fields, banned phrases and similarity. A judge model only rules on semantic claims about customers, and its precision is measured on a labeled set.

9Assumptions and what I changed

  • CRM is HubSpot (the design is identical on Salesforce). Forms post webhooks. Sequencer is Outreach or Apollo.
  • Target accounts have one named owner; non-target leads route by territory (US West / Central / East + Canadian provinces).
  • "Fleet size" means power units; US carriers can later be checked against FMCSA public data.
  • Providers, reverse-IP, Slack and the sequencer are mocked. LLM outputs were generated once and stored; every check on them runs live.
  • The 7-day dashboard is ~210 simulated form fills scored by the real engine; rep response times and enrichment hit rates are assumptions.
  • Added, unasked: shadow mode, the rep feedback loop, the "find the buyer" step for signal-only accounts, CASL consent for Canada, and fail-open/fail-closed asymmetry.
  • Changed: scoring is the prototype rather than routing, because routing is only as good as the score it reads.
